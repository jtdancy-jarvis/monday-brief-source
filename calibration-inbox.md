## ARCHIVE

### 2026-08-05 — Name a show everyone liked that left you cold. (Asked 2026-08-03)

**Answer:** Succession, and Breaking Bad / Better Call Saul. Mad Men and The Bear
were not named.

**Follow-up, same session:** Of Fargo, Slow Horses, and The Bear — only Slow
Horses works. Comfort rewatch is Scrubs, not Ted Lasso. Nonfiction subjects that
override the voice test: Disney/park design and golf. Golf scope: architecture
and travel/destination, not player profiles or tour coverage. Rope before
bailing: two or three episodes. Books: both audiobook and print.

**What it changed.** Rules 1 and 2 rewritten in shared memory:

- **Rule 1 is now "warmth is wit plus loyalty, not sentiment."** Scrubs over Ted
  Lasso and Slow Horses over The Bear both point the same way — humor is the
  delivery mechanism for warmth, and openly earnest is tolerated rather than
  targeted.
- **Rule 2 is now "the bail signal is moral descent and mutual contempt."** Not
  darkness. Succession is very funny and still failed because nobody in it likes
  anyone; Breaking Bad failed because the characters get worse; Mad Men is colder
  than either and survived.
- **Antihero prestige drama excluded by default** as a genre (Sopranos, Ozark,
  Billions and relatives).
- **New primary lane: comic ensembles with mutual loyalty**, promoted from an
  automatic flag.
- **New unifying lane: designed places** — Disney park design and golf course
  architecture treated as one interest.
- **Golf rescoped**; several existing picks are now partly off-scope.
- Two-or-three-episode rope, and audiobook/narrator noted on every book pick.

*Open follow-up: the wit-plus-loyalty model comes from six data points and hasn't
been tested against a live pick yet. Tracked as the `wit-loyalty-hypothesis`
thread.*
