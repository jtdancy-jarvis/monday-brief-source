## TASTE THROUGH-LINE

Seven rules, in priority order. When two conflict, the lower number wins.

1. **Warmth beats craft.** The bail signal is tone, not pacing or plot. A cold,
   airless, technically accomplished thing gets abandoned; a flawed warm one
   gets finished. This is the single most predictive rule in the file.
   *(Confirmed 2026-08-03.)*

2. **Voice is the criterion.** For books especially: is the narrator good
   company? Judge tone, never genre label.

3. **Mid and lighter.** Real stakes are fine. Relentless bleakness, cruelty as
   texture, and grimness for its own sake are not. It is a 6am commute.
   *(Confirmed 2026-08-03.)*

4. **Automatic flags.** Bill Lawrence projects. Comic ensemble mysteries. Golf
   writing. These get pitched whenever they exist, and they strengthen any
   subscription case they appear in.

5. **Mostly new, one catalog slot.** Default to what is out now. Reserve exactly
   one pick per episode for something older worth going back for.
   *(Confirmed 2026-08-03.)*

6. **No book quota.** Fiction/nonfiction split is whatever is strongest that
   week. Two novels or two histories are both fine. *(Confirmed 2026-08-03.)*

7. **TV counts as much as film.** Home viewing by default. Theater only for
   genuine events.

## HARD EXCLUSIONS

Never pitch: AI books, political insider books, self-help, romantasy, horror,
austere literary fiction, dry sci-fi.

Rule 1 extends the austere-literary-fiction exclusion to **all media**. A cold
prestige drama is the same failure mode as a cold novel. Known examples to avoid
recommending: Severance, Silo, and anything whose main selling point is
atmosphere over people.

Fully caught up on Thursday Murder Club. Never pitch book one.

## STREAMING TIERS

- **Tier 1** — Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, Prime. Recommend freely.
- **Tier 2** — Apple TV+, HBO Max. Conviction picks only, and always make the
  one-billing-month case: name three or four things on that service worth the
  same month, then say to cancel.
- **Tier 3** — Peacock, Paramount+, MGM+. Skip.

**Verify availability before recommending.** Never state a service from memory.

## PODCASTS

Register: **enthusiast deep-dive.** Hosts with real expertise going long on
something they love. Not celebrity interviews. Length is never a reason to skip.

Already in rotation, do not recommend: Acquired, The Disney Dish, The Golfer's
Journal.

## AI AND TECH

**Minimized as of 2026-08-03.** Include only when a story genuinely clears the
bar. Cap at roughly 150 words. Skip the segment entirely on an ordinary week
rather than filling it.

Clears the bar: a real capability jump with verifiable evidence; a major safety
or security incident; regulation that actually binds; a chip or company move
that reshapes the market.

Does not clear the bar: incremental model releases, funding rounds, product
updates, benchmark scores, executive shuffles, anything an aggregator is excited
about.

## QUESTION BANK

Two per episode, or one per day via the daily calibration task. Rotate
categories. Never repeat within eight weeks — check the refinement log.

**Tone and warmth**
- Name a show everyone liked that left you cold. What was missing?
- Is there a comedy you keep returning to, and what does it do that others don't?
- When something is sad, do you want it earned and quiet, or big and cathartic?
- Does bleak work if it's funny, or does funny not rescue bleak?

**Structure and commitment**
- Do you prefer a finished series you can see the end of, or something ongoing?
- Miniseries, or many seasons?
- How many episodes will you give something before quitting?
- Do you rewatch, and if so what?

**Books**
- Last book you finished in under a week, and what drove that?
- Last book you abandoned, and where exactly did you stop?
- Do you read at night, on the commute, or in stretches at the weekend?
- Audiobook or print by default, and does the narrator make or break it?
- Do you prefer a standalone or a series you can settle into?
- Is there a nonfiction subject you'd read about regardless of the writer?

**Film**
- Last film worth the time, and was it new or a rewatch?
- Do you want a film pick every week, or only when there's a real one?
- Any director whose name alone is enough?

**Sports**
- How much UNC detail do you want in the offseason, honestly?
- Do you want golf coverage when you're not playing much?
- Should the Panthers get real coverage in season or stay a clause?

**Disney**
- Parks history and Imagineering, or current news and booking windows?
- Is a trip on the horizon, and should the segment plan around it?

**Format**
- Is the runtime right, or would you take longer?
- Which segment do you skip when you're not in the mood?
- Which segment would you expand if only one could grow?
