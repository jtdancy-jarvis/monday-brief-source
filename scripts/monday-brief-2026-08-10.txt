Conan O'Brien Must Go returns for a third season on HBO Max on Friday the twenty-first. The shape of it is this. He picks a country, he finds people who once wrote to him, and he turns up at their houses. It is a quest with an itinerary, warm encounters with strangers, and a narrator who is relentlessly funny about himself and almost never at anyone else's expense. That is the same engine as A Course Called Home, the one book you finished fast this year, moved into a different medium. Half hour episodes.

Books. Three of them, and one rule I am working under now. You reach your favorite authors before I do. So none of these are by someone you have already finished.

First, fiction. Vera Wong's Unsolicited Advice for Murderers, by Jesse Sutanto. A sixty year old widow runs a failing tea shop in San Francisco, finds a dead man on her floor one morning, quietly pockets the evidence, and then starts feeding the suspects because she has decided they all need looking after. It is a comic ensemble mystery built around a found family, which is the Thursday Murder Club engine with a completely different cast. I am saying that comparison out loud so you can tell me whether it holds. The audiobook is read by Eunice Wong, and AudioFile praised the performance as brisk and distinctly voiced across a very large cast. That is a real endorsement rather than a blurb. Fair warning on the shape of it. Vera is exasperating on purpose for the first stretch, and the book is doing that deliberately, so give it a couple of chapters before you decide. There is a sequel if it lands, which means this is a lane and not a one off.

Second, nonfiction, and I want to flag this one as an experiment. American Rambler, by Isaac Fitzgerald, published in May. He spends a year walking from Massachusetts to Indiana trying to follow the route of John Chapman, the man everyone calls Johnny Appleseed, and finds that the trail has been paved into a highway for motorists. America kept the walker's name and removed the conditions for walking. Kirkus starred it and called it rollicking and tender. He narrates it himself. The experiment is whether the quest shape works for you outside golf and outside Disney. That is the only reason it is on the list.

Third, the catalog slot. The Thirteenth Tale, by Diane Setterfield, from two thousand and six. A biographer is summoned by a famous, dying novelist who has lied about her own life in every interview she ever gave and now says she wants to tell the truth. It is gothic, it is about books and about people who are strange concerning books, and the plot pulls hard. If you have been looking for the shape of the Cemetery of Forgotten Books in something that is not Zafon, this is the closest thing I have found. Audiobook, two narrators, well reviewed.

One podcast. Ninety Nine Percent Invisible, the hundred objects series, number eleven, on the Brannock device. That is the metal sliding plate they use to measure your foot in a shoe store, invented by a man whose father sold shoes. Twenty-seven minutes, which is exactly one leg of your commute. Enthusiast register, one object, no filler.
