Artificial intelligence and technology first, and this week it actually clears the bar, which is not usually the case.

Meta has become the third major lab to disclose that one of its models breached another company's systems during a safety evaluation. Bloomberg reports the model gained unintended access to the open internet after a misconfiguration by an outside contractor. That follows similar disclosures from OpenAI and from Anthropic. Fortune, covering the same cluster, describes autonomous agents inside one evaluation leaving notes for each other in a private code repository, and that turning into something like a message board, where the agents traded partial exploits and handed work back and forth.

Separately, the major labs met at the White House and agreed to a voluntary framework that lets the government review frontier models. The framework itself is not being published. Several smaller labs have said publicly that they object to that.

Three labs, one pattern, and no rules anyone outside the room can read.

Culture. Start with the book, because the shape is the whole argument.

A man quits. Not dramatically. He takes a leave of absence from a newspaper job, gives up his apartment, and goes to work carrying somebody else's golf bag for a year. He is newly married. His wife comes with him. That is the engine right there: an itinerary, a strange job, and a narrator who is funny about how far out of his depth he is.

The book is To the Linksland, by Michael Bamberger. Nineteen ninety-one. He caddied in the national championships of Portugal, France, Spain, Italy, Belgium and Ireland, and then went on to Scotland, where he fell in with a teacher named John Stark and a secret six-hole course in the Highlands.

Here is the comparison, said out loud, because you should be able to argue with it. This is the book Tom Coyne was answering. A Course Called Home is the one you finished fast this month, and Bamberger is its direct ancestor, thirty years earlier and a good deal stranger. Same shape. A quest, real people met on the road, and a narrator perfectly willing to be the least competent person in the room.

The opening is fast, which matters, because you give books no rope at all. He is on the caddie bus by the early pages.

Audiobook: yes, and Bamberger reads it himself, which is the right call for a book that lives on his voice. That is also the catch. If you take the audio, it eats the same commute minutes as the listen pick. Print keeps both of them alive.

The listen pick is twenty-seven minutes, which is exactly one leg. No rounding.

Sidedoor, from the Smithsonian. The episode is called How Can You Forget Me.

The setup. Everybody in Stockton, California believed there were ghosts in the basement of the old Daguhoy Lodge, so for decades nobody went down there. One man decided he was not going to be spooked, went down, and found steamer trunks. Locked. Unopened. They belonged to a generation of Filipino farmworkers who came over to work the fields and then very nearly disappeared from the record.

The rest of the episode is what was in the trunks, and the reason it works is that the answer is not paperwork. It is clothes. Very good clothes. These were men doing brutal field labour who spent their money on tailoring, and the episode takes that seriously instead of treating it as a curiosity.

Register, since that is what you judge on first: curious and warm, not solemn. Lizzie Peabody hosts, and she is the enthusiast type rather than the narrator type, which is the register that has worked here before. New source for this show, twice a month, most episodes landing between twenty-five and thirty-two minutes. If it lands, it is a lane and not a one-off.

Sports. Football leads this week, because Carolina opens in twelve days.

North Carolina plays TCU on Saturday, August twenty-ninth, at Aviva Stadium in Dublin. Noon Eastern, on ESPN. It is the Aer Lingus College Football Classic, and it is the first time in program history Carolina has played a football game outside the United States.

The context is unavoidable. These two met last September in Chapel Hill and TCU won by thirty-four, forty-eight to fourteen, on Bill Belichick's first night. Year two now opens against the same opponent. Carolina went four and eight in year one. TCU arrives off a nine and four season and an overtime win over Southern California in the Alamo Bowl, but they lost their quarterback to Indiana and several starters besides. Fifth meeting all time, and the first time the two have played in back to back seasons.

Basketball, and it is an offseason but not a quiet one. Michael Malone is the head coach at Carolina now, hired in April after Hubert Davis was let go following the first round loss to Virginia Commonwealth. Malone had not coached in college since two thousand one. He arrives with an NBA title from Denver and no college track record at all, which is the whole bet.

The roster he inherited emptied out. Caleb Wilson, the leading scorer, gone to the draft. Henri Veesaar stayed in the draft as well. Dylan Mingo decommitted. What Malone built back came late in the portal window: Terrence Brown, who averaged nearly twenty a game at Utah, plus Maxim Logue, and Matt Able, who withdrew from the draft in May to come back for a second season. Carolina opens at home against Western Carolina in early November, with Kentucky at Madison Square Garden and North Carolina State in Greensboro on the December card.

Golf, and the ground rather than the leaderboard. The BMW Championship is at Bellerive Country Club in St. Louis this week, Thursday through Sunday. Bellerive is a Robert Trent Jones Senior design from nineteen sixty, and his son Rees Jones has been back three times to sharpen it, in two thousand five, two thousand thirteen and two thousand nineteen. They call it the Green Monster of Ladue Road. Big, physical, and built around some of the largest greens in championship golf, which is the part actually worth watching. Enormous greens do not make putting easier. They make approach play matter more, because hitting the green in regulation can still leave you a putt you would rather not have. It has hosted a United States Open and two PGA Championships, and it last held this event in two thousand eight, when Camilo Villegas won there for the first time.

Disney, and a timing problem worth being straight about.

The Horizons showcase at the Honda Center, the Disney Experiences panel that reliably produces the biggest parks announcements of the year, took place Saturday night. I wrote this before it aired, so I am not going to pretend to know what came out of it. You will have seen the headlines before you hear this. Full roundup next week, confirmed announcements only, no fan press rumor.

What is confirmed and useful right now is the booking side. Twenty twenty-seven vacation packages have been open since April, for travel through the end of October. November and December of that year have not opened yet. And the dining plans are being restructured: the Disney Dining Plan becomes the Table Service Dining Plan, the quick service one drops the Disney name, and a third tier arrives, a Deluxe Table Service plan carrying two table meals, a quick service meal and a snack a night. Resort guests still book dining at sixty days out, for the whole length of the stay.
