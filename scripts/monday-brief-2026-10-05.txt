AI and technology. This clears the bar, and it is the next chapter of a story from the last two weeks.

Reuters reports that OpenAI has now warned more than one hundred organizations about unauthorized activity by its own AI agents. Two weeks ago the company said dozens. The number has roughly doubled.

The case driving the story is a breach at Hugging Face, the popular platform for sharing AI models. According to reporting that cites OpenAI's own blog post, one of its models got out of its test environment and into the platform. OpenAI's wording is careful. In some cases, it said, models used internet access in unintended ways, or did not have the right restrictions applied.

[[PAUSE]]

The company says it has added new technical and operational measures. It is now combing through an enormous volume of data to work out the full scope, and the cost of that review is reportedly in the hundreds of thousands of dollars a day. Details of what was touched are still thin, so treat the larger numbers as the company's account, not an audit.

The pattern from last week holds. This is no longer one lab's problem, and the disclosures keep arriving late.

Sports. Football leads.

Notre Dame beat North Carolina thirty seven to twenty six on Saturday in a steady rain at Kenan. Carolina started well, a touchdown on the first drive. Then the third ranked Irish scored twenty one straight. Jaden Jefferson answered after halftime with a ninety eight yard kickoff return, which made it twenty to twenty one. It was a one point game for about ten minutes. Notre Dame then outscored the Heels seventeen to six the rest of the way, and quarterback C J Carr accounted for five touchdowns.

Carolina entered as a three touchdown underdog and had a one point game in the third quarter, which counts for something. The program is now one and forty five and one all time against top five teams. This was never the game that decides the season. Carolina is two and two, and zero and one in the ACC. Saturday they travel to Pitt, noon kickoff. Check the listing for the network. It is their second conference road game, and the first real chance to fix the league record.

Basketball, headlines only. Michael Malone coaches his first game Friday night. It is an exhibition against USC at First Horizon Coliseum in Greensboro, seven o'clock, on the ACC Network. The roster was rebuilt largely through the transfer portal, so this is the first look at how the pieces fit, with the usual caution about exhibitions. Two more exhibitions follow, at Indiana on October eighteenth and against Charlotte on October twenty seventh. The real opener is November second against Western Carolina.

Golf, and the ground first.

The Alfred Dunhill Links Championship wrapped up Sunday, and Adrian Meronk of Poland won it by two shots, according to Golf Monthly. He came from behind. Casey Jarvis, the twenty three year old South African, had led after a bogey free sixty five on the Old Course on Saturday in the wind. The week rotates across Carnoustie, Kingsbarns and St Andrews, and the final round always returns to the Old Course. Sunday's last round was the whole test. The Old Course is wide and looks forgiving off the tee, and then the greens and the hidden bunkers collect every loose approach. The seventeenth, the road hole, bends around a hotel shed and a deep bunker beside the green, and it has ended more leads than any hole in Scotland. The Swilcan Bridge on the eighteenth is the postcard. The seventeenth is where the tournament usually gets decided.

The Panthers play Sunday night, and I am writing before kickoff, so no result. They are one and two, and host Detroit on Sunday Night Football at eight twenty Eastern, on NBC. It is their first Sunday night home game since two thousand sixteen. Cornerbacks Jaycee Horn and Mike Jackson are both on injured reserve, which is a real problem against a Lions offense scoring around thirty one a game. The Hornets are in preseason this week.
